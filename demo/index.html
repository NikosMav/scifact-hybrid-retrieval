Claim 399 · Exposure to fine particulate air pollution is relate to anxiety prevalence.

  1. OBJECTIVE To determine whether higher past exposure to particulate air pollution is associated with prevalent high symptoms of anxiety.
  2. DESIGN Observational cohort study.
  3. SETTING Nurses' Health Study.
  4. PARTICIPANTS 71,271 women enrolled in the Nurses' Health Study residing throughout the contiguous United States who had valid estimates on exposure to particulate matter for at least one exposure period of interest and data on anxiety symptoms.
  5. MAIN OUTCOME MEASURES Meaningfully high symptoms of anxiety, defined as a score of 6 points or greater on the phobic anxiety subscale of the Crown-Crisp index, administered in 2004.
  6. RESULTS The 71,271 eligible women were aged between 57 and 85 years (mean 70 years) at the time of assessment of anxiety symptoms, with a prevalence of high anxiety symptoms of 15%.
  7. Exposure to particulate matter was characterized using estimated average exposure to particulate matter <2.5 μm in diameter (PM2.5) and 2.5 to 10 μm in diameter (PM2.5-10) in the one month, three months, six months, one year, and 15 years prior to assessment of anxiety symptoms, and residential distance to the nearest major road two years prior to assessment.
  8. Significantly increased odds of high anxiety symptoms were observed with higher exposure to PM2.5 for multiple averaging periods (for example, odds ratio per 10 µg/m(3) increase in prior one month average PM2.5: 1.12, 95% confidence interval 1.06 to 1.19; in prior 12 month average PM2.5: 1.15, 1.06 to 1.26).
  9. Models including multiple exposure windows suggested short term averaging periods were more relevant than long term averaging periods.
  10. There was no association between anxiety and exposure to PM2.5-10.
  11. Residential proximity to major roads was not related to anxiety symptoms in a dose dependent manner.
  12. CONCLUSIONS Exposure to fine particulate matter (PM2.5) was associated with high symptoms of anxiety, with more recent exposures potentially more relevant than more distant exposures.
  13. Research evaluating whether reductions in exposure to ambient PM2.5 would reduce the population level burden of clinically relevant symptoms of anxiety is warranted.

Claim 137 · Asymptomatic visual impairment screening in elderly populations does not lead to improved vision.

  1. OBJECTIVE To assess whether population screening for impaired vision among older people in the community leads to improvements in vision.
  2. DESIGN Systematic review of randomised controlled trials of population screening in the community that included any assessment of vision or visual function with at least 6 months' follow up.
  3. SUBJECTS Adults aged 65 or over.
  4. MAIN OUTCOME MEASURE Proportions with visual impairment in intervention and control groups with any method of assessing visual impairment.
  5. RESULTS There were no trials that primarily assessed visual screening.
  6. Outcome data on vision were available for 3494 people in five trials of multiphasic assessment.
  7. All the trials used self reported measures for vision impairment, both as screening tools and as outcome measures.
  8. The inclusion of a visual screening component in the assessment did not result in improvements in self reported visual problems (pooled odds ratio 1.04:95% confidence interval 0.89 to 1.22).
  9. A small reduction (11%) in the number of older people with self reported visual problems cannot be excluded.
  10. CONCLUSIONS Screening of asymptomatic older people in the community is not justified on present evidence.
  11. Visual impairment in this age group can usually be reduced with treatment.
  12. It is unclear why no benefit was seen.
  13. Further work is needed to clarify what interventions are appropriate for older people with unreported impairment of vision.
